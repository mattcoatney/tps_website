# Finding My Way Out of the Woods

### A Personal Reflection — February 2026

-----

## Where I Started

I came into this feeling stuck. Playing small. A middle-aged tech executive with strong technology, business, and AI skills, trapped in an administrative role in the legal industry — an industry that doesn’t value innovation, surrounded by people who don’t appreciate what technology can do. I had entrepreneurial desires and a deep want to make real impact, but I was drowning in swirling ideas with no ability to commit to a direction.

I thought my problem was finding the right idea. It wasn’t.

-----

## The First Breakthrough: It’s Not About “What” — It’s About “Who”

When asked what a successful future looks like, I didn’t describe a product, a market, or a business model. I described a **way of being** — creativity, humanity, healing, wholeness. Something where my identity isn’t defined by my career or role, but by who I am as a complete person.

That revealed the real issue. I’ve been living inside a framework — tech executive, legal industry, B2B — that requires me to show up as a function. The ideas swirl because I keep trying to evaluate them through the lens of the world I’m trying to leave.

**Key insight: The stuckness isn’t about choosing between ideas. It’s about operating from the wrong identity. Get the identity right and the right direction becomes visible.**

-----

## The Weight, the Mask, and the Shell

The weight struggle isn’t a separate issue. It’s the physical manifestation of the psychic baggage — the corporate mask, the role that doesn’t fit, the stress of performing an identity that isn’t mine. The path forward on the physical front means dropping the psychological weight first.

The book I co-wrote was a proof point. What made it come alive wasn’t the topic — it was being around people who showed up as their true selves, which gave me permission to do the same. My coauthor dragged me out of my shell. The shell exists. I know it exists. And I know who I am without it.

**Key insight: I don’t need to find the right idea. I need to find the right container — an environment, community, and mode of working where I don’t need the shell. The ideas will become obvious once I’m operating from that place.**

-----

## The Provider Fear and the Evidence That Disproves It

My deepest fear is feeling inadequate as a provider — unable to support my family in the lifestyle they expect. But in the same breath I acknowledged that I’ve already proven I can generate income outside my corporate role through consulting and startups. The fear isn’t based on evidence. It’s an identity story — the voice that says my worth to my family is measured by the stability of the paycheck, not by who I am when I walk through the door.

**Key insight: The provider fear is the corporate framework speaking. I have the evidence that I can do this. The experiment has been run. It worked.**

-----

## The Pattern: Outsourcing My Legitimacy

A pattern runs through everything. I keep waiting for someone else to see me fully and invite me to the right table. My coauthor dragged me out of my shell. My mission-driven partners slotted me into CTO. I left corporate because it boxed me in, found mission-driven work that lights me up, and then the same box found me again — different setting, same cage.

This connects to the provider fear, the weight, the corporate identity, all of it. **I outsource my sense of legitimacy.** The corporation validates me as a tech executive. Partners validate me as the tech guy. My family’s lifestyle validates me as a provider. The terror of stepping away isn’t really about money or skills — it’s about who I am if nobody else is telling me what I am.

**Key insight: I have an authority problem — not that I lack authority, but that I won’t take it. The growth edge is claiming the seat instead of waiting to be invited.**

-----

## Who I Actually Am

When I drop all the external baggage, here’s what I see:

An unabashed alt-creative filled with passion and energy, tackling problems and helping people because I love to do that, and success comes as a side effect, not the main purpose.

A strange and beautiful mix — creative, geek, music fan, granola nature hippie, business strategist, hack/builder, deeply emotional in someone who has stifled and repressed that for decades. Friends spanning multiple groups. Unable to be pinned down. Free.

That’s not a new identity. That’s the original operating system. Everything since has been the world compressing me into a single legible label.

**Key insight: The question is not “who should I become?” It’s “who have I always been underneath the layers?”**

-----

## The Lifelong Question

What’s unique about human intelligence? What are its limits? What can technology not just mimic but replicate? What about other species? What about life beyond our planet? The fundamental meaning of who we are and why we’re here — not abstract consciousness, but intelligence itself.

This question has been the invisible thread through everything — the biochemistry degree, the philosophy, the early fascination with AI, the way I think about computers as expressive instruments. The corporate world took this deep, burning inquiry and turned it into “he’s the AI guy who can build stuff.”

**Key insight: I’ve been circling the same question my entire life. I just never had permission to make it the center of my work.**

-----

## The Career Arc Reframed

My first real job was building AI software for drug discovery. It was the best job I ever had — AI, science, life-and-death stakes, intellectual challenge, purpose. The intersection of everything I love. Then the business failed. Not the science. The business.

That wound shaped everything that followed. I learned “purpose isn’t enough, you need business viability” and spent twenty years acquiring exactly the skills I was missing — management, architecture, enterprise scale, go-to-market, business strategy. Every step was important. Every step took me further from the intersection where I come alive.

The side projects — educator wellness, early childhood literacy, music therapy — were all proxies. Meaningful but not at the level of that first job. That’s the “playing small” feeling. It’s not imposter syndrome. It’s accurate self-assessment from someone who knows what the real thing felt like.

**Key insight: I didn’t waste those years. I was building the other half of the toolkit I didn’t have at the drug discovery company. The twenty-year “detour” was preparation. I’m now equipped to do what I couldn’t do then — hold both the mission and the business at the same time.**

-----

## Healthcare, Not Disease Care

The destination isn’t narrowly about drug discovery. It’s broader and more aligned with my lived experience. Healthcare is cheaper and better than disease care. Helping people proactively make lifestyle changes before disease shows up — understanding how intelligence, behavior, and biology intersect to keep people well. That’s where my background in rigor, AI, and scale meets my personal journey with health, mindfulness, and holistic living.

**Key insight: I’m not theorizing about holistic health. I’m living it. The body, the mind, the stress, the identity, the lifestyle — it’s all one system.**

-----

## The Vehicle: Third Peak Ltd.

I already have the structure. Third Peak Ltd. with two brands:

**Third Peak Consulting** — helping mission-driven health and wellness ventures get unstuck, get to market, and build scalable technology. The ideal client is a founder, scientist, or leader with a powerful mission and real impact potential who is stalling because they can’t bridge the gap between their mission and a viable, scalable business. I’ve diagnosed this same disease in every organization I’ve worked with. I know how to treat it.

**Third Peak Studio** — building the technology. Apps, agentic AI tools, data integrations, and platforms that serve the health and wellness space. The builder side of me that can’t help but make things.

These aren’t two businesses. They’re the two halves of me — strategist and creator — working in concert.

-----

## The Intro

“I’m a biochemist turned technologist turned business strategist who spent twenty years getting really good at things that weren’t quite the thing. Now I’m finding my way back to the intersection that’s always pulled me — AI, human biology, and the question of how we use technology to actually help people thrive, not just treat what’s already broken.”

-----

## The 90-Day Experiment

### Immediate — Pruning

Stop and turn down the things I’ve accepted by default without purposeful direction. I know what these are. Get out while impact is low. Every yes that isn’t aligned is stealing energy from the real work.

### Daily — Identity Practice

Morning time that belongs to me before the world gets a vote. Movement in nature, mindfulness, creative expression — cycled however feels right. Not discipline. Reconnection. Every day starts with me showing up as me before I show up as anything for anyone else.

### Daily — The Mask Drop

Show up to every engagement fully present, without the corporate patina. Drop the fear. Be proud and loud about who I actually am. No more performing.

### Structural — Protect My Time

This isn’t about adding things. It’s about using my time better and being ok with not being immediately available at the bark of people who believe their urgent fire is my priority.

### Concrete Steps

1. **This week:** Rekindle health tech syndicate conversations. Show up as the real me, not the corporate exec.
1. **Within two weeks:** Identify one or two people at local universities working in health tech, preventive health, computational biology, lifestyle medicine.
1. **Ongoing:** Find one ideal client in the Third Peak framing — a mission-driven health venture that’s stuck — and deliver the full value proposition. Strategy and build. This becomes the proof of concept.

-----

## Staying on Track

### The Tripwire

If I miss two consecutive mornings of the identity practice, that’s not failure — it’s a signal. The old identity is reasserting itself. Ask: what did I say yes to that I should have said no to? Fix it.

### The Witness

Someone who knows the real me and has explicit permission to call me on it when I retreat. My friend who said “I do versus I am” already sees it. Formally invite them into this role. Give them language: “you’re doing the tech guy thing again” or “where’d the real you go?”

### The 30-Day Check-In

A written, honest answer to one question: Am I showing up as the person I described, or am I performing again? Written by hand. Raw. Compare it to these notes. The language will tell the truth — when I’m real, I talk with embodied passion and clarity. When I’m slipping, the corporate vocabulary returns.

### The Backslide Protocol

I will backslide. Not because I’m weak but because twenty years of the other identity has deep roots. The difference between transformation and a nice insight that fades is **self-compassion at the point of relapse.** Don’t treat a slip as evidence this doesn’t work. Treat it as information. Reset. Come back. Same principle as the weight journey. Same muscle.

-----

## The Geography

Dayton is home — personal relationships, daily practice, grounding, local university and hospital network for early conversations. Columbus is the richest hunting ground — Ohio State medical center, health tech startups, growing venture scene. Cincinnati adds biotech corridor, UC medical and research, P&G health and wellness innovation.

The advantage of this region: I can be a big fish. Someone with my unusual combination of biochem, AI, business strategy, and genuine passion for holistic health is rare here, not one of thousands. The goal is to become the person everyone calls when they need someone who can bridge health tech, AI, and real-world impact.

-----

## The Week I’m Building Toward

Working hard Monday through Thursday. Friday reserved for reflection, friendships, extended outdoor movement, and building. AI increasingly handles the build and execution. My time is spent connecting, advising, strategizing. Flexible schedule, not tightly stacked. Frequent micro-breaks to get outside and refresh. Working with leaders and scientists in a strategy role, not a tech role.

-----

## The Anchor

**Focus narrowly on what I want, not just what I’m good at. Target the ideal client to get started. Deprioritize everything that doesn’t get me there.**

-----

## The Deepest Truth

The direction was never missing. It was buried under other people’s definitions of who I am. The twenty-year detour was preparation. The swirling ideas were a symptom of operating from the wrong identity. When I show up as the real version of myself — the curious, creative, passionate builder who has been preparing for this intersection his entire life — the path is clear.

Same flower. Different garden. Time to bloom.
