# Third Peak Website Redesign Brief

## Purpose
Redesign the existing Third Peak website so it feels less like generic AI consulting and more like a focused, credible advisory firm for practical business leaders. The site should resonate with owner-led and CEO-led Midwest operating businesses that want clear, actionable guidance on AI without hype, fear-mongering, or a bloated consulting engagement.

The site should communicate:
- calm executive credibility
- practical clarity over jargon
- confidence without chest-thumping
- human, approachable tone
- selective, premium positioning
- strong initial emphasis on the flagship offer: **AI Reality Check**

This is not a tech agency website, not a futurist/media site, and not a generic transformation consultancy. It should feel like an experienced operator helping leaders make smart decisions and move forward with confidence.

---

## Brand and positioning

### Company name
Use **Third Peak** as the primary brand.

Preferred presentation:
- **Third Peak**
- Descriptor beneath or alongside the logo: **Executive guidance for practical AI strategy**

Avoid leading with:
- consulting jargon
- digital transformation language
- innovation theatre
- “we build anything” language
- agency-style “services” framing

### Core positioning
Third Peak helps practical business leaders cut through AI confusion, avoid wasted spend, and identify a small number of high-value moves they can act on now.

### Primary audience
Owner-led and CEO-led Midwest operating businesses, roughly:
- $10M-$100M revenue
- 30-300 employees
- healthcare-adjacent or operationally complex SMBs
- not software companies
- not large enterprises
- not traditional professional services firms

### Buyer mindset
The ideal buyer is thinking:
- “I know AI matters, but I can’t tell what is real.”
- “I don’t want to get left behind.”
- “I also don’t want to waste money chasing hype.”
- “My team is dabbling, but there is no real plan.”
- “I need clarity, prioritization, and a path forward.”

---

## Overall design direction

### Tone and visual feel
The site should feel:
- premium but not flashy
- natural, grounded, modern
- executive and strategic, but still human
- confident, clear, and calm

Think:
- less glossy corporate AI startup
- less generic management consulting
- more trusted guide for important decisions

### Design principles
- Clean, spacious layout
- Strong typography and hierarchy
- Short sections with high scanability
- Limited but meaningful animation
- Rich whitespace
- Minimal clutter
- Warm but restrained color palette
- Use light visual structure rather than heavy boxes everywhere

### Visual inspiration
Visual tone should suggest:
- clarity
- direction
- momentum
- grounded leadership
- subtle outdoors / horizon / path / summit sensibility, but do not make the site feel like an outdoor brand

### Avoid
- neon AI aesthetics
- glowing circuit graphics
- robots, humanoid AI imagery, generic tech meshes
- stock photos of coders in hoodies
- excessive gradients or startup cliché visuals
- too much text density
- giant consultant-style paragraphs

---

## Suggested style system

### Color palette direction
Use a restrained, credible palette with one subtle accent.

Suggested direction:
- Deep navy / slate for primary text and key blocks
- Warm off-white / soft stone background
- Muted sage, steel blue, or deep green accent
- Optional sand or warm gray secondary neutral

This should feel grounded, mature, and modern rather than loud or trendy.

### Typography direction
Use a strong, readable modern type system.

Suggested pairing approach:
- Headings: elegant modern sans serif with authority
- Body: clean, highly readable sans serif

Priorities:
- excellent readability
- strong hierarchy
- short line lengths
- punchy headlines
- no overly geometric or playful fonts

### UI style
- rounded corners, but subtle
- refined cards where helpful
- light borders
- soft shadows only when needed
- understated iconography
- no heavy visual chrome

### Goal
Immediately communicate who this is for, what problem is solved, and what kind of engagement this is.

### Layout
- left: headline, subheadline, 1-2 CTA buttons
- right: simple visual or structured content panel
- optional trust signal line below CTAs

### Headline options
Use one of these or a refined variation:

**Option A**
Practical AI clarity for business leaders who need a real path forward.

**Option B**
Cut through AI confusion. Find the moves that actually matter.

**Option C**
Clear, executive-level guidance for practical AI decisions.

### Subheadline draft
Third Peak helps owner-led and CEO-led businesses cut through AI hype, avoid wasted spend, and move forward with a practical 90-day roadmap grounded in real business outcomes.

### CTA options
Primary:
- Start with an AI Reality Check
- Schedule a conversation

Secondary:
- See how it works
- Explore offerings

### Hero support panel idea
Instead of an abstract image, use a structured panel showing the 3 value pillars:
- **Clarity** — what matters, what does not
- **Prioritization** — where to focus first
- **Momentum** — a practical 90-day path

Alternative visual:
A subtle abstract landscape / horizon / path motif that implies direction and steadiness.

## 2. Problem / tension section

### Goal
Show the buyer that you understand the exact mess they are in.

### Section headline
AI feels urgent. That does not make it clear.

### Body copy
Many leaders know AI matters. What they do not have is a grounded way to decide where it fits, what is hype, where the risk lives, and what to do first.

Without that clarity, companies tend to drift into one of two traps:
- too much experimentation and no real progress
- too much hesitation and no real movement

Third Peak helps leaders avoid both.

### Optional visual treatment
Two-column tension block:
- **Trap 1: scattered experimentation**
- **Trap 2: staying stuck**
Then a middle line or callout:
- **The goal: focused, practical momentum**

## 3. Who we help

### Goal
Make the ICP feel seen and subtly disqualify bad fits.

### Section headline
Built for practical operators, not AI theatre.

### Copy draft
Third Peak works best with owner-led and CEO-led businesses that know AI could matter, but need a grounded way to evaluate where it can improve operations, support growth, and reduce friction without creating unnecessary risk or distraction.

### Best-fit bullets
Best fit:
- Midwest operating businesses
- healthcare-adjacent organizations
- admin-heavy, coordination-heavy, operationally complex SMBs
- leaders who want clarity and forward motion, not a giant consulting project

### Not-for-everyone block
Not the right fit for:
- software companies with deep in-house AI teams
- large enterprises looking for broad transformation programs
- organizations seeking a slide deck with no intent to act
- buyers looking for cheap implementation labor

Use this section to reinforce selectivity and premium positioning.

## 4. Offerings overview

### Goal
Show a focused set of offerings, with AI Reality Check clearly spotlighted.

### Section headline
Focused ways we help.

### Structure
Create 3 offering cards.

### Card 1 — featured
**AI Reality Check**
A short, fixed-fee engagement that helps leadership understand where AI can create value, where it can create risk, and what to do in the next 90 days.

Button:
- See the AI Reality Check

### Card 2
**AI Product and Launch Advisory**
For leaders who need to shape, pressure-test, or accelerate an AI-enabled product, capability, or internal tool without overbuilding or overcommitting.

### Card 3
**Executive Technology and AI Advisory**
Ongoing strategic guidance for leaders facing important technology, product, or AI decisions and needing experienced judgment without a full-time hire.

Important: keep the other two offerings present but secondary. The site should visually and verbally anchor on AI Reality Check.

## 5. Spotlight section for AI Reality Check

### Goal
Make this the anchor offer and most tangible entry point.

### Section headline
Start with an AI Reality Check.

### Intro copy
A focused executive engagement designed to help leadership teams make sense of AI quickly and practically. In a short, fixed-fee sprint, we identify where AI can create real value in your business, where it is likely to waste time or money, and what your team should do next.

### Suggested layout
Use a 2-column layout:
- left: what it is and why it matters
- right: deliverables or process card

### What they get
- Executive AI brief tailored to the business
- Opportunity map across revenue, efficiency, and operations
- Ranked priorities based on value, effort, readiness, and risk
- Clear view of dead ends, traps, and likely wasted-spend areas
- 90-day roadmap
- First use case blueprint ready for pilot planning or vendor evaluation

### Process
Use a simple 4-step horizontal or vertical sequence:
1. Understand
2. Assess
3. Focus
4. Deliver

#### Supporting microcopy
**Understand**
Clarify goals, pressure points, and current experimentation.

**Assess**
Evaluate practical use cases, readiness, risk, and likely value.

**Focus**
Align on the few moves that actually matter.

**Deliver**
Leave with a roadmap and one concrete next step ready to move.

### CTA
- Start the conversation
- Ask about an AI Reality Check

## 6. Why Third Peak

### Goal
Explain why you are credible without sounding like a résumé dump.

### Section headline
Experienced judgment. Practical approach.

### Copy direction
This section should position Third Peak as grounded in real executive and operating experience, not theory.

### Copy draft
Third Peak brings executive-level technology, product, and AI judgment to leaders who need clarity and traction. The perspective behind the work comes from years of navigating complex technology decisions, building and advising teams, supporting founders and operators, and helping organizations avoid expensive mistakes while moving toward meaningful outcomes.

This is not about AI for its own sake. It is about using it thoughtfully, practically, and in ways that support the business.

### Suggested proof point structure
Use 3-4 concise proof cards, for example:
- Helped leaders avoid expensive technology and AI missteps
- Guided product and technology decisions in both startup and operating environments
- Strong rapport with non-technical leaders and founder-led teams
- Combines executive judgment, technical depth, and a human, practical style

Do not make the page feel like a solo freelancer résumé. Position as a firm with senior-led experience.

### Goal
Show believable value without overclaiming.

### Section headline
What practical progress looks like.

### Format

If you later have more specific case studies, replace these with tighter, metrics-backed blurbs.

## Tone and writing rules

### Tone
- crisp
- grounded
- human
- executive
- practical
- calm
- selective

### Writing rules
- short paragraphs
- avoid long blocks of copy
- use plain English
- avoid jargon and buzzwords
- avoid consultant fluff
- avoid overclaiming
- avoid sounding salesy
- write like an experienced operator speaking to another practical leader

### Words and phrases to avoid
- digital transformation
- unlock synergies
- leverage cutting-edge AI
- end-to-end solutions
- world-class
- paradigm shift
- innovation journey
- seamless integration
- bespoke at every turn

### Preferred language
- clarity
- practical
- grounded
- momentum
- path forward
- real value
- avoid wasted spend
- meaningful outcomes
- confident decisions
- focused next steps

## UX notes

- Make the homepage immediately understandable within 5 seconds
- Keep primary CTA visible in header and hero
- Use section spacing generously
- Use concise cards and content blocks to improve scanning
- Mobile experience should stay clean and not become text-heavy
- Ensure each major section can stand on its own visually
- Keep animations subtle and minimal

---

## Image and illustration direction

Use imagery sparingly.

Preferred image styles:
- subtle landscape / horizon / path abstraction
- modern editorial textures
- restrained photography of real business environments if needed
- diagrams or structured visual blocks instead of decorative AI visuals

Avoid:
- robotic AI imagery
- overly literal mountain photography everywhere
- obvious stock handshake photos
- generic office diversity stock

A few carefully chosen, quiet visuals will work better than many.

## What success looks like
The redesigned site should make the right visitor think:
- “This feels grounded.”
- “This person understands the business side, not just the technology.”
- “This is focused and credible.”
- “I could talk to them without getting sold a giant consulting project.”
- “This feels like clarity and momentum, not hype.”

The site should make the wrong visitor think:
- “This is probably not for us.”

That is a feature, not a bug.
