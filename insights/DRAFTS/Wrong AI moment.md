"Most Organizations Are Preparing for the Wrong AI Moment"
OpenAI reportedly believes its next model could meaningfully accelerate the economy — not improve it incrementally. Actually accelerate it.

That's not hype if you've been watching closely. Frontier models are already writing production code, compressing hours of research into minutes, and automating workflows that used to require entire teams.

Most organizations are still preparing for the "AI efficiency" moment — chatbots, summarization, basic workflow automation. That's reasonable. It's also already yesterday's problem.

The next wave isn't about doing existing things faster. It's about the rapid obsolescence of current operating models — and the emergence of ones we haven't fully imagined yet.

The organizations that will navigate this well aren't the ones with the biggest AI budgets. They're the ones comfortable operating in uncertainty — running experiments before the playbook exists, iterating on half-formed hypotheses, building the capacity to change direction fast.

That's not a technology problem. It's a design thinking problem.

The question worth asking right now: Is your organization built to learn faster than the technology is changing? If not, no amount of AI investment will help.
